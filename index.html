Industry Context

Who Offers
Workplace Charging?

The Reality
A Cost They Can Absorb — Until They Can't
Companies like Google, Salesforce, and Microsoft have offered free workplace charging for years as a strategic talent perk. For a firm generating hundreds of millions in annual revenue per employee, a $100K/year electricity bill is negligible.

But as EV adoption grows and more employees plug in, that cost scales rapidly — and smart CFOs are starting to notice.
The Tipping Point: When 5–10% of employees drive EVs, costs are manageable. When 20–30% do — the projected near-future — unmanaged charging becomes a real budget line.

The 20–80% Battery Health Rule
Why Partial Charging Is Actually Optimal
Lithium-ion batteries experience chemical stress at extreme charge states. EV manufacturers including Ford, VW, Tesla, and BMW all recommend daily charging between 20–80% for longevity.

Geotab's analysis of 22,700 EVs found that batteries spending prolonged time above 80% SOC degrade at 2.0% per year vs. 1.4% per year for those staying within the 20–80% window.

A workplace session averaging 21.8 kWh — taking a car from ~15% to ~48% — sits perfectly within the optimal zone.

Demand Pattern Analysis

Understanding the
Peak Demand Anatomy

⚡ Simulated kW Demand — Typical Campus Day
The 2–4 PM spike is post-lunch behavior: employees return from lunch, plug in for the afternoon, and these sessions naturally run into the 4–9 PM peak pricing window — a double cost impact.
Event 1 — 8–10 AM
🌅 Morning Plug-In Rush
Employees arrive and plug in simultaneously. Creates a sharp kW spike that can hit the demand charge threshold. However, morning sessions often end by 2 PM — staying off-peak.
Event 2 — 2–4 PM ← Most Dangerous
☕ Post-Lunch Plug-In Event
Employees return from lunch and plug in. These off-peak start sessions average 6+ hours — extending well into the 4–9 PM peak window. Both energy rate and demand charge impact.
Event 3 — 4–9 PM
💰 Peak Billing Window
PG&E charges 2.6× the off-peak rate during this window. Any session running through here — especially long afternoon sessions — drives disproportionate energy costs on the BEV meter.
